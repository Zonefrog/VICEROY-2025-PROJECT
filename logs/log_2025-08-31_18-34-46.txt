- Greek drama originated from the Dionysia festival and was closely tied to religious rituals and storytelling. It served as a form of worship and entertainment for the ancient Greeks.
- Notable playwrights of ancient Greek theater include Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, and Aristophanes, each contributing significantly to the development of Greek drama.

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the historical context and significance of the Dionysia festival in the birth of Greek theater.
- Recognize the connection between religious ceremonies and the origins of Greek drama.
- Identify key playwrights and their contributions to ancient Greek theater.

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Watch clips of ancient Greek theater performances to observe the style and structure of the plays.
- Analyze the differences in structure between a Greek tragedy and a Greek comedy to understand the conventions of each genre.

Real-world applications and examples:
- Compare the themes and storytelling techniques of ancient Greek theater with modern theater productions to draw parallels between the two.

Sub-topic: The Greek Chorus and Its Role in Theater

- The Greek chorus was a group of actors who sang and danced while providing commentary on the events of the play. They represented the voice of society and offered moral reflections on the actions of the characters.
- The chorus evolved from originally having 50 members to smaller groups of 12 or 15 members. This change allowed for more individual character development within the chorus.
- The role of the chorus in ancient Greek theater was to provide context, commentary, and emotional depth to the play, enhancing the overall narrative and engaging the audience.

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the significance of the Greek chorus in ancient Greek theater and its role in the performance.
- Analyze how the chorus contributed to the storytelling and emotional impact of a play.
- Identify the evolution of the Greek chorus from larger groups to smaller, more focused ensembles.

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Create a modern interpretation of a Greek chorus performance by incorporating music, movement, and spoken word.
- Write a commentary on a contemporary play as if you were part of the Greek chorus, providing insights and reflections on the characters and plot.

Real-world applications and examples:
- Explore the use of ensemble storytelling in modern theater productions by examining how contemporary plays utilize group performances to enhance the narrative and engage the audience.

Sub-topic: Roman Theater and Its Influence on Greek Theater

- Roman theater was heavily influenced by Greek theater but had distinct features such as the use of music, dance, and elaborate costumes. Roman plays were often adaptations of Greek works.
- The architectural design of Roman theaters differed from Greek amphitheaters, with semi-circular or full-circle layouts and raised stage areas. The seating arrangements were based on social class, with the best seats reserved for the elite.
- Roman theater had a lasting impact on the development of theater as a whole, influencing the design of theaters and the types of performances that were popular.

Learning Objectives:
- Recognize the influence of Roman theater on the development of theater practices and architectural design.
- Identify key differences between Greek and Roman theatrical traditions in terms of performance styles and audience engagement.
- Understand the legacy of Roman theater in modern-day theater architecture and performance conventions.

Week 2: Acting Techniques in Theater

Main Topic Title: Stanislavski and Meisner Techniques

Sub-topic: The Stanislavski Method and Its Principles

- The Stanislavski Method, developed by Konstantin Stanislavski, is a set of acting techniques focused on emotional authenticity, realistic character portrayal, and psychological exploration.
- Actors using the Stanislavski Method aim to create believable and truthful performances by drawing on their own experiences and emotions to connect with their characters.
- The method emphasizes the importance of understanding the character's motivations, objectives, and inner life to deliver a convincing and compelling performance.

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the foundational principles of the Stanislavski Method and its impact on modern acting techniques.
- Apply emotional authenticity and psychological exploration to character development to create more nuanced and realistic performances.
- Analyze how the Stanislavski Method enhances an actor's ability to connect with their character and the audience.

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Practice emotional memory exercises from the Stanislavski Method to develop a deeper connection to the character's emotions and experiences.
- Analyze a scene using the principles of the Stanislavski Method to identify character motivations, objectives, and emotional arcs.

Real-world applications and examples:
- Apply the Stanislavski Method to contemporary acting roles by exploring how actors use emotional authenticity and psychological exploration to bring characters to life in film, television, and theater productions.

Sub-topic: The Meisner Technique and Its Key Exercises

- The Meisner Technique, developed by Sanford Meisner, focuses on truthful response to imaginary circumstances by living truthfully under given circumstances.
- Actors using the Meisner Technique engage in exercises such as repetition, where they repeat phrases to establish a connection and respond instinctively to their scene partner.
- The technique emphasizes the importance of listening, observing, and responding truthfully to create authentic and spontaneous performances.

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the core principles of the Meisner Technique and its focus on truthful response and spontaneous acting.
- Develop the ability to respond truthfully and instinctively to imaginary circumstances to create dynamic and engaging performances.
- Identify key exercises and techniques used in the Meisner Technique to enhance an actor's responsiveness and presence on stage.

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Practice the repetition exercise with a partner to improve listening skills, responsiveness, and emotional connection.
- Perform a scene using the Meisner Technique of living truthfully under given circumstances to experience the spontaneity and authenticity it brings to the performance.

Real-world applications and examples:
- Explore the use of the Meisner Technique in film and television acting to understand how actors apply the principles of truthful response and spontaneity in on-screen performances.

Sub-topic: Brechtian Theater and Epic Theater Techniques

- Brechtian theater, developed by Bertolt Brecht, aims to provoke critical thought in the audience by disrupting emotional identification with the characters and encouraging analytical thinking.
- Techniques used in Brechtian theater include breaking the fourth wall, direct address to the audience, and the alienation effect, which prevents the audience from becoming emotionally invested in the performance.
- Epic theater, a style associated with Brecht, focuses on social and political themes, uses non-linear narratives, and incorporates songs, projections, and other multimedia elements to engage and challenge the audience.

Learning Objectives:
- Identify the key characteristics of Brechtian and epic theater and how they aim to provoke critical reflection in the audience.
- Analyze how techniques such as breaking the fourth wall and the alienation effect are used to disrupt traditional theatrical conventions and engage the audience intellectually.
- Explore the legacy of Brechtian theater in modern political and social commentary in theater, film, and other forms of performance art.

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Create a short scene using Brechtian techniques, such as direct address to the audience or breaking the fourth wall, to explore the impact of these techniques on storytelling and audience engagement.
- Discuss and analyze the use of the alienation effect in a contemporary play to understand how it challenges traditional theatrical conventions and encourages critical thinking.

Real-world applications and examples:
- Examine the influence of Brechtian theater on modern political and social commentary in theater by studying how contemporary playwrights and directors use similar techniques to engage audiences in critical reflection and dialogue.
