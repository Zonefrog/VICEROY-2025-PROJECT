Week 1: LLM-Content Benchmarking Datasets

Topic 1: General Language Understanding Evaluation (GLUE) Benchmark
- Sub-topics:
  1. Overview of the GLUE benchmark and its importance in evaluating language models.
  2. Description of the nine different tasks included in the GLUE benchmark.
  3. Analysis of the performance metrics used in the GLUE benchmark tasks.

- Explanation:
This sub-topic will provide students with a comprehensive understanding of the GLUE benchmark, its tasks, and the metrics used to evaluate language models' performance based on the benchmark.

- Learning objectives:
  - Understand the purpose and significance of the GLUE benchmark in evaluating language models.
  - Identify the different tasks included in the GLUE benchmark and their characteristics.
  - Analyze the performance metrics used to assess language models on the GLUE benchmark tasks.

- Estimated number of slides: 8
- Lab activities:
  - Task: Analyze a sample GLUE benchmark task and evaluate a language model's performance.
  - Objective: Gain hands-on experience in assessing language models using the GLUE benchmark tasks.

- Real-world applications:
  - The GLUE benchmark is widely used in research and industry to evaluate and compare the performance of various language models across different natural language understanding tasks.

  - Assess the generalization capabilities of language models through experimentation with the CC-News dataset.

  - Objective: Gain practical experience in using the CC-News dataset for LLM-content detection tasks.

- Real-world applications:
  - The CC-News dataset serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners to test and enhance the performance of language models in detecting LLM-content within news articles.

Week 2: LLM-Content Detection and Evading Techniques

Topic 3: Detecting Toxic Content with the Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset
- Sub-topics:
  1. Overview of the Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset and its significance in evaluating language models' performance in detecting toxic or harmful content.
  2. Analysis of the toxicity levels assigned to Wikipedia comments in the dataset.
  3. Examination of the labeling process and criteria for toxicity in the Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset.

- Explanation:
This sub-topic will focus on the characteristics of the Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset, its role in assessing language models' ability to detect toxic content, and the labeling methodology used in the dataset.

- Learning objectives:
  - Understand the purpose of the Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset in evaluating language models for toxic content detection.
  - Interpret the toxicity levels assigned to comments in the dataset and their implications.
  - Analyze the criteria and process for labeling toxicity in the Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset.

- Estimated number of slides: 7
- Lab activities:
  - Task: Label a sample of Wikipedia comments for toxicity levels and compare the results with the dataset labels.
  - Objective: Gain hands-on experience in assessing toxic content detection using the Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset.

- Real-world applications:
  - The Wikipedia Toxicity Dataset is instrumental in training and evaluating language models to identify and mitigate toxic or harmful content in online platforms.

Topic 4: Evading LLM Detectors through Adversarial Attacks
- Sub-topics:
  1. Understanding adversarial attacks on language models and their implications for evading LLM detectors.
  2. Exploring strategies such as data poisoning and model inversion to deceive LLM detectors.
  3. Analyzing the impact of adversarial attacks on the reliability and robustness of language models.

- Explanation:
This sub-topic will cover the concept of adversarial attacks on language models, the techniques used to evade LLM detectors, and the consequences of such attacks on the models' performance.

- Learning objectives:
  - Define adversarial attacks on language models and their role in evading LLM detectors.
  - Identify strategies like data poisoning and model inversion to manipulate language models.
  - Evaluate the implications of adversarial attacks on the reliability and effectiveness of language models.

- Estimated number of slides: 9
- Lab activities:
  - Task: Create adversarial examples to deceive an LLM detector and observe the model's response.
  - Objective: Gain practical insights into the vulnerabilities of language models to adversarial attacks and evasion techniques.

- Real-world applications:
  - Adversarial attacks pose a significant threat to the integrity and security of language models, highlighting the importance of developing robust defenses against such attacks.
