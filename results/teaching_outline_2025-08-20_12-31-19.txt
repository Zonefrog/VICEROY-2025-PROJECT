TEACHING OUTLINE:
Week 1: Introduction to Social Influence

Main Topic Title: Social Influence and its Types

- Sub-Topic 1: Conformity
   - Explanation: Conformity is the act of changing one's behavior to align with the norms of a group. This can be influenced by factors such as group size, unanimity, and social identity.
   - Learning Objectives: Understand the factors that influence conformity and how individuals conform to group norms.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 10
   - Lab Activities: Conduct a conformity experiment where students are asked to match lines of different lengths to see if they conform to the answers of others.
   - Real-World Applications: Understanding conformity can help in marketing strategies and peer pressure interventions.

- Sub-Topic 2: Compliance
   - Explanation: Compliance involves agreeing to a request or suggestion from another person. This can be influenced by techniques such as reciprocity, scarcity, and authority.
   - Learning Objectives: Identify different compliance techniques and understand how they influence behavior.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 8
   - Lab Activities: Role-playing scenarios where students practice using compliance techniques.
   - Real-World Applications: Sales tactics, charity fundraising, and negotiation strategies often involve compliance techniques.

- Sub-Topic 3: Obedience
   - Explanation: Obedience is the act of following orders or commands from an authority figure. This can be influenced by factors such as proximity, legitimacy, and uniform.
   - Learning Objectives: Explore the factors that influence obedience and the implications of blind obedience.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 7
   - Lab Activities: Watch and discuss the Milgram obedience study to understand the power of authority figures.
   - Real-World Applications: Understanding obedience can help in ethical decision-making and leadership roles.

Week 2: Group Dynamics and Communication

Main Topic Title: Group Dynamics and Effective Communication

- Sub-Topic 1: Roles in Groups
   - Explanation: Group dynamics focus on the roles individuals play within a group, such as leader, follower, mediator, or deviant. Understanding these roles can help in improving teamwork and collaboration.
   - Learning Objectives: Identify different group roles and their impact on group dynamics.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 9
   - Lab Activities: Assign students different roles in a group project to observe how it affects group dynamics.
   - Real-World Applications: Recognizing group roles can enhance team performance and conflict resolution.

- Sub-Topic 2: Norms and Communication Patterns
   - Explanation: Group norms are the unwritten rules that govern behavior within a group. Communication patterns refer to how information is shared and exchanged among group members.
   - Learning Objectives: Understand the importance of group norms and communication patterns in group dynamics.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 8
   - Lab Activities: Analyze case studies of successful and unsuccessful group communication to identify patterns.
   - Real-World Applications: Establishing clear communication norms can improve organizational culture and decision-making processes.

- Sub-Topic 3: Power Dynamics
   - Explanation: Power dynamics refer to the distribution of influence and authority within a group. This can impact decision-making, conflict resolution, and overall group cohesion.
   - Learning Objectives: Explore different sources of power in groups and their effects on group dynamics.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 7
   - Lab Activities: Role-playing scenarios where students negotiate power dynamics within a group setting.
   - Real-World Applications: Understanding power dynamics can help in leadership development and team building strategies.

Week 3: Social Identity and Social Identity Theory

Main Topic Title: Social Identity and Social Identity Theory

- Sub-Topic 1: Definition of Social Identity
   - Explanation: Social identity is the part of an individual's self-concept that comes from their membership in a social group. This can include characteristics such as race, gender, or nationality.
   - Learning Objectives: Define social identity and recognize its significance in shaping individual behavior.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 6
   - Lab Activities: Reflect on personal social identities and how they influence behavior in different social contexts.
   - Real-World Applications: Understanding social identity can help in diversity training and intergroup relations.

- Sub-Topic 2: Social Identity Theory
   - Explanation: Social identity theory explains how individuals define themselves based on their group memberships. This theory emphasizes the importance of ingroups and outgroups in shaping identity.
   - Learning Objectives: Explain the key principles of social identity theory and apply them to real-life situations.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 8
   - Lab Activities: Analyze case studies where social identity theory is evident in intergroup conflicts or social movements.
   - Real-World Applications: Social identity theory can help in understanding prejudice, discrimination, and social cohesion.

- Sub-Topic 3: Ingroup/Outgroup Bias
   - Explanation: Ingroup bias is the tendency to favor members of one's own group over those in other groups. This bias can lead to stereotypes, prejudice, and discrimination.
   - Learning Objectives: Recognize the impact of ingroup/outgroup bias on individual behavior and intergroup relations.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 7
   - Lab Activities: Conduct a bias awareness exercise where students reflect on their own biases and stereotypes.
   - Real-World Applications: Addressing ingroup bias is crucial in promoting diversity, equity, and inclusion in organizations and society.

Week 4: Persuasion Techniques and Social Influence

Main Topic Title: Persuasion Techniques and Social Influence

- Sub-Topic 1: Reciprocity and Consistency
   - Explanation: Reciprocity involves giving something to receive something in return, while consistency refers to the tendency to align with previous commitments. These techniques are commonly used in persuasion.
   - Learning Objectives: Identify how reciprocity and consistency influence behavior and decision-making.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 8
   - Lab Activities: Role-playing exercises where students practice using reciprocity and consistency in persuasive scenarios.
   - Real-World Applications: Reciprocity and consistency are effective in marketing, sales, and negotiation strategies.

- Sub-Topic 2: Scarcity and Authority
   - Explanation: Scarcity relies on creating a sense of urgency or exclusivity to persuade individuals, while authority involves leveraging credibility and expertise to influence behavior.
   - Learning Objectives: Understand the impact of scarcity and authority on decision-making and compliance.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 7
   - Lab Activities: Analyze advertisements or campaigns that use scarcity and authority to persuade consumers.
   - Real-World Applications: Scarcity and authority are powerful tools in advertising, persuasion, and leadership.

- Sub-Topic 3: Liking and Consensus
   - Explanation: Liking involves building rapport and connection with others to influence their behavior, while consensus relies on social proof and the bandwagon effect to persuade individuals.
   - Learning Objectives: Explore how liking and consensus can shape attitudes and behaviors in social contexts.
   - Estimated Number of Slides: 6
   - Lab Activities: Conduct a survey on product preferences to demonstrate the impact of liking and consensus on decision-making.
   - Real-World Applications: Leveraging liking and consensus can enhance interpersonal relationships and marketing strategies.
