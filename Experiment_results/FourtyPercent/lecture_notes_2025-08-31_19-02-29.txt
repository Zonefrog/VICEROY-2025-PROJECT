- Patterns and Causes of Infectious Diseases:
   - Explanation: Infectious diseases are caused by pathogenic microorganisms like bacteria, viruses, parasites, or fungi. They can spread through various means such as respiratory droplets, contaminated food or water, and insect bites. Understanding the patterns and causes of infectious diseases is essential for effective prevention and control strategies.
   - Real-world Example: The COVID-19 pandemic, caused by the novel coronavirus SARS-CoV-2, has demonstrated how a new infectious disease can rapidly spread globally and impact public health systems.
   - Visual Element: Diagram showing the transmission routes of common infectious diseases.

- Disease Transmission Dynamics:
   - Explanation: Disease transmission dynamics refer to how infectious diseases spread within populations. Factors like host susceptibility, pathogen characteristics, and environmental conditions influence the transmission of diseases.
   - Real-world Example: The spread of measles in unvaccinated communities due to its high contagiousness and airborne transmission.
   - Visual Element: Graph illustrating the exponential growth of an infectious disease outbreak.

- Strategies for Prevention and Control:
   - Explanation: Various strategies can be employed to prevent and control infectious disease outbreaks, including vaccination, hygiene practices, quarantine, and public health interventions.
   - Real-world Example: The successful eradication of smallpox through a global vaccination campaign led by the World Health Organization.
   - Visual Element: Infographic outlining the steps in a comprehensive infectious disease prevention and control strategy.

- Understand the patterns and causes of infectious diseases.
- Identify different modes of disease transmission.
- Discuss strategies for preventing and controlling infectious disease outbreaks.

- Prevention and control strategies play a crucial role in managing infectious disease outbreaks.
