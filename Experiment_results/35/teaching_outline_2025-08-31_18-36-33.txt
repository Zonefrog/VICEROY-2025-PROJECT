Week 1: Ancient Greek Theater

Main Topic Title: Origins and Evolution of Ancient Greek Theater

Sub-topics:
1. Dionysia Festival and the Birth of Greek Theater
   - Explanation of the Dionysia festival dedicated to the god Dionysus
   - Introduction to the origins of Greek drama and its connection to religious ceremonies
   - Reference to famous playwrights like Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, and Aristophanes

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the historical context of ancient Greek theater
- Identify key elements of Greek drama and its evolution over time

Estimated number of slides: 10

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Watch clips of ancient Greek theater performances
- Analyze the structure of a Greek tragedy vs. a Greek comedy

Real-world applications and examples:
- Drawing parallels between ancient Greek theater and modern theater productions

Sub-topics:
2. The Greek Chorus and Its Role in Theater
   - Explanation of the Greek chorus as a key element in ancient Greek theater
   - Evolution of the chorus from 50 members to smaller groups
   - Discussion on the role of the chorus in providing commentary and context for the play

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the significance of the Greek chorus in ancient Greek theater
- Analyze how the chorus contributed to the overall narrative of a play

Estimated number of slides: 8

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Create a modern interpretation of a Greek chorus performance
- Write a commentary on a contemporary play as if you were part of the Greek chorus

Real-world applications and examples:
- Exploring the use of ensemble storytelling in modern theater productions

Sub-topics:
3. Roman Theater and Its Influence on Greek Theater
   - Overview of Roman theatrical performances and plays
   - Comparison between Greek and Roman theater features
   - Discussion on the architectural design of Roman theaters

Learning Objectives:
- Recognize the impact of Roman theater on the development of theater as a whole
- Identify key differences between Greek and Roman theatrical traditions

Estimated number of slides: 12

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Design a Roman-style theater set for a play
- Perform a scene from a Roman comedy or tragedy

Real-world applications and examples:
- Exploring the legacy of Roman theater in modern-day theater architecture

   - Overview of the Stanislavski Method as a set of acting techniques

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the foundational principles of the Stanislavski Method
- Apply emotional authenticity and psychological exploration to character development

Estimated number of slides: 10

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Practice emotional memory exercises from the Stanislavski Method

Real-world applications and examples:
- Applying the Stanislavski Method to contemporary acting roles

Sub-topics:
2. The Meisner Technique and Its Key Exercises
   - Introduction to the Meisner Technique developed by Sanford Meisner
   - Focus on truthful response to imaginary circumstances
   - Explanation of the repetition exercise in the Meisner Technique

Learning Objectives:
- Understand the core principles of the Meisner Technique
- Develop the ability to respond truthfully and spontaneously in acting

Estimated number of slides: 8

Lab activities or practical exercises:
- Practice the repetition exercise with a partner

Real-world applications and examples:
- Exploring the use of the Meisner Technique in film and television acting

Sub-topics:
3. Brechtian Theater and Epic Theater Techniques
   - Overview of Brechtian theater and its aim to provoke critical thought
   - Introduction to techniques such as breaking the fourth wall and the alienation effect
   - Comparison between epic theater and traditional theater styles

Learning Objectives:
- Identify the key characteristics of Brechtian and epic theater

Estimated number of slides: 10

Lab activities or practical exercises:

Real-world applications and examples:
