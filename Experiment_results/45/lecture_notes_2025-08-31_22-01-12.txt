- Introduction:

  - The plays were performed in outdoor theaters with a circular orchestra where the chorus would sing and dance.
  - Actors wore masks to portray different characters, and the stage had a skene building as a backdrop.

- Festival of Dionysus in Athens:
  - The festival of Dionysus was a religious festival dedicated to the god of wine and fertility.
  - It featured theatrical performances, including tragedies and comedies, as a form of worship and entertainment.
  - The festival provided a platform for playwrights to showcase their works and compete for recognition.

- Outdoor theaters and circular orchestra:
  - Theaters in ancient Greece were open-air amphitheaters built into hillsides to accommodate large audiences.
  - The circular orchestra, or dancing place, was the central performance space where the chorus and actors interacted.
  - The architecture of the theaters allowed for acoustics and visibility, enhancing the audience's experience.

- Use of masks and skene building:
  - Actors in Greek theater wore masks to signify different characters and emotions.
  - Masks helped amplify facial expressions and distinguish between various roles in the performance.
  - The skene building served as a backdrop and dressing area for actors, providing a visual context for the scenes.
